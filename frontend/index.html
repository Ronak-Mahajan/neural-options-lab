Example Pricing a hypothetical contract.

Price an option and check the quote against simulation.

Set the contract in the sidebar, or type a ticker to use a real one. Everything reprices as you change it, and a fresh simulation of the same model checks the network's price on every change.

The Model price card shows the pricer's output: one forward pass through a five-network ensemble, with the five Greeks taken as exact derivatives of that pass by automatic differentiation. The Monte Carlo price beside it re-simulates the same contract on a new seed, with a 95% interval, using the engine that produced the network's training labels. Above 12 trading days that is the control-variate Asian engine; at or below, it is the rough-volatility engine the short-dated network was distilled from. Cross-check, beneath it, is the gap between the two prices in basis points of the strike. It measures how closely the network reproduces its reference engine. It does not test the model's assumptions about the market.

The contract is an arithmetic-average Asian option above 12 trading days. At or below 12 trading days a second model trained on rough volatility takes over and prices a standard European option. The price jumps across that boundary because the two sides are different contracts under different dynamics.

No price on this page is a market price, and none is quotable. The short-dated model's rough-volatility parameters and the Hedge tab's simulated market are fitted to SPY option quotes captured on 20 August 2026. Every other input is hypothetical unless you load a ticker in the sidebar. The Hedge tab sells a 30-day at-the-money call and hedges it daily under transaction costs, then reports the tail loss of a learned policy and of two textbook rules on the same paths.

Method and checks

Integrated Gradients against an at-the-money baseline option. The contributions plus the baseline price reproduce the quoted price; the residual is the discretisation error of the integral.

Measured accuracy of the model price A standing benchmark for the averaged-contract pricer, measured once on held-out contracts; it does not change with the inputs. The short-dated model's measured error is on the methodology page.

Pricing accuracy for the averaged contract, out of sample

Measured against high-precision simulation on averaged contracts held out of training and drawn from the same parameter box, so this is the model's error inside that box. The short-dated model is measured separately, on the methodology page.

Short-dated volatility surface

A model smile for expiries up to 12 trading days, from a surface trained with butterfly and calendar-spread penalties. Both conditions are re-checked by automatic differentiation at every point shown, and the result is reported below for this grid. The surface is a constrained fit to the short-dated pricing model and is no more accurate than that model. It depends on volatility and rate only, so it does not move with the contract in the sidebar.

Hedge a short call and see what it costs you.

Sell one 30-day at-the-money call and hedge it daily, paying a transaction cost on every trade. A learned policy, a Black-Scholes delta hedge and a cost-aware Whalley-Wilmott band run on the same 15,000 simulated paths. The panel reports each one's worst-5% loss, average P&L and trading cost per path.
