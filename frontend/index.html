Example Pricing a hypothetical contract.

A neural network prices an exotic option in milliseconds, with exact Greeks.

Every number on this page is computed live for the contract in the sidebar. The network's price is checked against a fresh Monte Carlo simulation of the same contract on every change.

The Network price card is one forward pass through a five-model ensemble, which also returns the five Greeks as exact derivatives. The Monte Carlo price card is a fresh simulation of the same contract, with a 95% confidence interval. The Agreement card in the middle is the test that matters: the two should differ by less than the simulation's own error bar.

The contract is an arithmetic-average Asian option above 12 trading days. At or below 12 trading days a second model trained on rough volatility takes over and prices a standard European option, so the price jumps across that boundary: they are different contracts under different dynamics.

Nothing here is market data unless you load a ticker in the sidebar. The Hedging tab runs a separate experiment: a learned hedging policy against delta hedging, under transaction costs.

Method and checks

Integrated Gradients against an at-the-money baseline option. The four contributions sum to the price; the residual is the completeness error.
