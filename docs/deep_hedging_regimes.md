# Deep hedging as a designed experiment: rough volatility, jumps and costs

Generated by `scripts/deep_hedging_regimes.py`; every number here was measured in that run (`docs/deep_hedging_regimes.json` holds them all, `docs/deep_hedging_regimes.png` is the figure).

## Question

`docs/hedging_findings.md` records that on risk-neutral GBM the learned CVaR hedger loses to a vol-matched delta hedge and to Whalley-Wilmott. Under GBM with small costs that is expected: the market is complete and a static delta is near-optimal. This experiment asks whether the learned policy wins where a static delta cannot - rough stochastic volatility with spot-vol correlation, jumps (an incomplete market) and transaction costs - against three properly specified baselines on identical paths.

## Protocol

- Contract: short one 30-day ATM European call, S0 = K = 1, 30 daily rebalances (dt = 1/252), CVaR_95 of the terminal loss (positive = loss) with the premium booked at the Monte Carlo price under the evaluation measure (40,000-path probe).
- Measures (parameters read from disk, none hardcoded):
  - `gbm`: sigma = sqrt(xi_SPY) = 0.1130, r = 0.0371.
    Realized terminal vol 0.1128, premium 180.3 +/- 1.2 bp, terminal-log-return skew -0.01, kurtosis 3.0.
  - `rbergomi`: eta = 3.9352, rho = -0.5363, H = 0.2613, xi = 0.012773 (sigma = 0.1130), r = 0.0371; source `rough_calibration.json`.
    Realized terminal vol 0.1150, premium 148.8 +/- 0.9 bp, terminal-log-return skew -3.71, kurtosis 52.8.
  - `rbergomi_jumps`: eta = 3.9179, rho = -0.5464, H = 0.2498, xi = 0.013802 (sigma = 0.1175), r = 0.0371, jumps (lam, mu_j, sig_j) = (0.848, -0.0241, 0.0117) per step, compensated; source `_fit_jumps_last.json (rate from rough_calibration.json)`.
    Realized terminal vol 0.1220, premium 157.6 +/- 1.0 bp, terminal-log-return skew -3.70, kurtosis 53.1.
  The SPY calibration is the one its own quality gate marks `accepted: false` (eta pinned at its 4.0 bound); it is used as specified because it is the calibration the repository has, and the rejection is a caveat on the measure, not on the comparison.
- Baselines, all vol-matched to the realized terminal vol of the evaluation measure: (i) Black-Scholes delta; (ii) Whalley-Wilmott no-trade band with the risk aversion chosen on a separate 20,000-path tuning set from the grid [0.1, 0.3, 1.0, 3.0, 10.0, 30.0, 100.0] to minimise the baseline's own CVaR (in-sample for the baseline, deliberately generous); (iii) the Ruf-Wang linear-regression hedge h = c0 + c1 delta + c2 delta(1 - delta), coefficients fitted by OLS to minimise the variance of cost-free terminal P&L on the same 20,000 tuning paths (P&L is linear in c without costs, so OLS is exact), then charged costs on the test paths.
- Deep policies: the shipped `hedger_gbm.pt` for the GBM-trained row, and two new checkpoints trained with the same recipe (CVaR_95 objective, AdamW, cosine schedule, batch 2048) under each rough measure with sigma and rate pinned at the calibrated level and the cost sampled from [0, 0.02]:
  - `hedger_gbm.pt`: measure `gbm`, 6000 iters x batch 2048, 133.1 s of training.
  - `hedger_rbergomi.pt`: measure `rbergomi`, 4000 iters x batch 2048, 866.9 s of training.
  - `hedger_rbergomi_jumps.pt`: measure `rbergomi_jumps`, 4000 iters x batch 2048, 876.2 s of training.
- Evaluation grid: train measure x eval measure x cost ['0', '10 bp', '50 bp', '100 bp', '200 bp'], 3000 paths x 5 seeds per cell, 500 bootstrap resamples. Ratios deep/baseline are bootstrapped PAIRED (both hedgers on the same resampled paths). 'Wins' = ratio below 1 by more than 2 SE; 'loses' = above 1 by more than 2 SE.

### What the CVaR gap is made of

CVaR_95 of the loss splits exactly into the mean loss and the CVaR_95 of the loss about its own mean, so each cell's advantage splits the same way. Taking the in-sample `rbergomi_jumps` cell at 50 bp, deep[rbergomi_jumps] versus the vol-matched delta: the CVaR advantage is 492.9 - 404.4 = **88.5 bp**, of which **78.5 bp is mean** and **10.0 bp is the demeaned tail**. The mean term is a commission bill: at 50 bp the delta hedge turns over 1.70 and pays 117.5 bp, the policy turns over 0.49 and pays 39.7 bp, a saving of 77.8 bp. The same split at 100 and 200 bp is 163 of 235 and 346 of 553 - the mean term is 69% and 62% of the advantage there.

Removing the mean from both sides changes the ranking. On demeaned CVaR_95 the tuned Whalley-Wilmott band is ahead of deep[rbergomi_jumps] at every cost below 200 bp under all three measures (for example 326.1 against 363.7 at 50 bp on `rbergomi_jumps`), and the policy's demeaned tail is the better one only at 200 bp under the two rough measures. The standard-deviation columns point the same way: deep[rbergomi_jumps]'s P&L is more dispersed than the delta hedge's in 11 of the 15 (measure, cost) cells, the exceptions being 100 and 200 bp under the two rough measures.

So the accurate statement of the result is that the deep policy attains the lower CVaR_95 in the rough-volatility and jump regimes once costs are 50 bp or more, and that it does so mostly by trading less rather than by reshaping the tail. The tail-shape term is real but small until costs reach 200 bp.

## Conclusion

Across all 135 (train x eval x baseline x cost) cells the deep hedger wins 51 by more than 2 SE and loses 75. In-sample (train = eval) versus the vol-matched delta: `gbm` median ratio 1.053, W2/L3 of 5; `rbergomi` median ratio 0.813, W3/L2 of 5; `rbergomi_jumps` median ratio 0.821, W3/L1 of 5.
Wins by baseline: 22 versus delta, 20 versus the linear hedge, 9 versus Whalley-Wilmott (each out of 45 cells).
The deep hedger beats every baseline, including the cost-aware Whalley-Wilmott band, in at least one cell; the tables above say which measure and cost, and the crossover table says from which cost on.
What that buys is stated precisely in "What the CVaR gap is made of": the policy has the lower CVaR_95 in the rough and jump regimes from 50 bp of cost on, and it gets there mostly by trading less - 89% of the 88.5 bp advantage in the in-sample `rbergomi_jumps` cell at 50 bp is a smaller commission bill, and on demeaned tail alone the Whalley-Wilmott band is still ahead below 200 bp.

## What would change this conclusion

- A cell where the paired ratio against Whalley-Wilmott is below 1 by more than 2 SE with a fresh set of seeds (17-21 were used here) would falsify a 'never wins' reading; a ratio above 1 by more than 2 SE on fresh seeds would falsify any 'wins' cell.
- The policy is the repository's 2-layer width-64 network with holdings clamped to [0, 1.5] and a conditional CVaR head, trained for the iterations listed above on CPU. A longer or larger training run that moved a losing cell to a win would be evidence against the policy, not against deep hedging.
- The rough measures are one calibration (its own gate rejects it for a pinned eta). A calibration with eta inside its bounds, or a jump fit with larger jumps than the SPY fit's 2.4% mean, changes the measure; the comparison should be rerun rather than extrapolated.
- The premium is booked at the Monte Carlo price under the simulated measure. Booking at Black-Scholes shifts every hedger's P&L in a table by the same constant, but that does not leave the ratios alone: CVaR is translation-equivariant, so a common shift moves numerator and denominator by the same absolute amount and the ratio moves. Priced at each measure's own realized terminal vol the shift is -2.3 bp on `gbm`, +32.1 bp on `rbergomi` and +32.9 bp on `rbergomi_jumps` - the rough measures' Monte Carlo premium sits well below Black-Scholes at the same terminal vol. Recomputing all 135 ratios on that booking moves them by a median of 0.015, and by up to 0.22 in the zero-cost `gbm` cells where the ratios are 3 to 6. No cell changes its W/L verdict and the crossover table is unchanged; the closest call is deep[rbergomi_jumps] versus delta on `rbergomi_jumps` at 10 bp, which goes from 1.058 +- 0.030 to 1.064 +- 0.033 against a two-SE loss line of 1.066. The shift is one constant inside every bootstrap resample, so the standard errors of the shifted ratios are the reported CVaR and ratio standard errors propagated through it rather than a fresh bootstrap; the margin on that closest cell, 0.002, is small enough that a rerun is what would settle it.
